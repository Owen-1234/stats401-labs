Global GDP Hierarchy

Two treemaps represent the same continent, area, and country hierarchy. Rectangle area encodes GDP in billions of U.S. dollars, while color encodes GDP status.

Design description

Rectangle area encodes GDP because a treemap uses a common spatial extent to support part-to-whole comparison across countries, areas, and continents. Color encodes GDP status as a categorical variable. Green marks increase, amber marks no change, and red marks decrease. The same scale and status palette are held constant across both figures, so their layouts can be compared without introducing a second visual explanation. Continent and area labels, nested boundaries, and full hierarchy paths in the tooltips make the parent-child structure explicit. The squarified treemap favors compact rectangles with relatively balanced aspect ratios. This generally improves country-label legibility and supports comparison among similarly sized economies. The slice-and-dice treemap alternates the partition direction across hierarchy levels. Its long bands make continental and regional nesting easier to trace, although small GDP values can produce narrow country cells and less readable labels. Together, the figures show how tiling changes spatial organization while preserving the same hierarchy, values, and status encoding.
