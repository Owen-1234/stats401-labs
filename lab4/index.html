Short analysis · 154 words

What the model and chart suggest

This study uses 50,001 public #ChatGPT tweets collected from January 22–24, 2023. I parsed timestamps and engagement fields, retained English posts, removed 224 repeated texts, and kept tweets matching exactly one transparent keyword group: Coding, Education, Work, or Reliability. The final tidy dataset contains 7,076 tweets. Sentiment was estimated from lightly normalized text with cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment-latest; sentiment score equals P(positive) minus P(negative). The left panel encodes each topic’s model-predicted negative, neutral, and positive shares. The right panel shows, for every topic–sentiment combination, the percentage of tweets receiving at least one like, reply, retweet, or quote. Reliability stands apart: 45.4% of its tweets are classified negative and only 15.3% positive. Coding is most positive, at 35.0%, while 69.3% of positive Coding tweets received an interaction. These associations are descriptive, not causal. Keyword groups can miss context, engagement is highly skewed, and RoBERTa predictions are estimates rather than ground-truth judgments.

Interpret with care

RoBERTa can misread sarcasm, slang, mixed sentiment, and domain-specific language. Keyword matching can also confuse a word’s topic or omit relevant posts. Results describe this short, hashtag-based collection and should not be generalized to all ChatGPT users.
