Data, method, and findings

Data and cleaning. This study uses 50,001 public #ChatGPT tweets collected from January 22–24, 2023. I parsed timestamps and engagement counts, retained English posts, removed 224 repeated texts, and excluded tweets that matched either none or multiple topic dictionaries. The resulting tidy dataset contains 7,076 tweets assigned uniquely to Coding, Education, Work, or Reliability.

Sentiment and encoding. I lightly normalized usernames and URLs, then applied cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment-latest to every retained tweet. The sentiment score is P(positive) minus P(negative). Panel A shows each topic’s negative, neutral, and positive composition. Panel B shows the percentage of each topic–sentiment group receiving at least one like, reply, retweet, or quote.

Findings. Reliability is the clearest outlier: 45.4% of its tweets are classified negative and 15.3% positive. Coding has the largest positive share (35.0%), and 69.3% of positive Coding tweets received an interaction. These patterns are descriptive, not causal: keyword matching can miss context, recorded engagement is highly skewed, and RoBERTa outputs are model estimates rather than ground-truth labels.
