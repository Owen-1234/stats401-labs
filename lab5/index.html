Design rationale

Node-link design

Color encodes district, symbol area shows passenger volume, and shape identifies station type. Route hue distinguishes service type. Width increases with travel time so long trips remain visible. Dragging reduces overlap. Highlighting isolates immediate neighbors, while tooltips report exact values.

Figure 2 · Adjacency matrix

District ordering reveals the network’s block structure

Rows and columns represent the same stations. Filled cells indicate direct routes. Color identifies route type and opacity represents travel time. Labels are grouped by district, then station type.

Figure 2. The symmetric pattern reflects an undirected network. Empty rows and columns identify isolates. Darker cells represent longer direct travel times, and the colored side bands show district membership.

Design rationale

Matrix design

Rows and columns share one station order. A filled cell denotes a direct route, hue shows route type, and opacity shows travel time. Ordering by district and station type creates comparison blocks. Label color preserves district membership. Passenger volume remains in tooltips because another cell encoding would reduce legibility.

Network questions and findings

What the two views support

Each conclusion identifies its evidential view and supporting encoding.

  1. Which stations appear central in the network?

    Answered wellBoth views

    Highlighting and occupied cells identify Stations 31 and 23 as central three-route stations. Station 31 also bridges distant sections.

  2. Which districts are strongly connected to one another?

    Answered wellMatrix

    District ordering supports block comparison. East–South is densest, followed by Central–North and East–West.

  3. Where are transfer or terminal stations located in the topology?

    Answered wellNode-link

    Shape locates station function. Terminals 4, 7, 10, 13, 16, 25, 31, and 34 are three-route junctions. Several later terminals are isolated.

  4. Which stations have high passenger volume?

    Answered wellNode-link

    Symbol area identifies Stations 50, 49, and 48 as highest-volume stations. Their peripheral locations separate volume from centrality.

  5. Where are the longest direct travel-time connections?

    Answered wellBoth views

    Link width and cell opacity identify 7–31 at 16 minutes, 18–34 at 15 minutes, and 29–50 at 14 minutes as the longest direct trips.

  6. Are particular route types concentrated in particular parts of the network?

    Partially answeredBoth views

    Route colors are dispersed across the network. Express includes the three longest routes, but no service type is confined to one region.
