Semantic findings

What the two structures reveal

1. Major semantic topics

The largest modeled topic is society, history and politics (153 passages), followed by quantitative and technical study (144) and degrees, transfer and services (125). The remaining topics include credit rules, academic procedures, policy, language, media, and life sciences. These labels summarize clusters after examining representative passages and TF-IDF terms.

2. Topics cross formal sections

Credits and placement contains 122 passages distributed across 50 formal sections in nine bulletin parts. The topic includes Degree Requirements, Grading and Grade Requirements, and Entrance Credit and Placement. Its spread shows that a single semantic theme is not confined to a single heading.

3. Sections vary in semantic diversity

Among sections with at least ten retained passages, Social Science (SOSC) has the highest topic entropy at 2.12 bits, with 11 passages spread across five topics. Global Health (GLHLTH) also spans five topics among 15 passages. Both combine disciplinary content that the formal heading alone does not convey.

4. Related policies appear in different parts

and both explain approval of outside coursework for DKU credit. Their original embedding vectors have cosine similarity 0.87, although the passages appear in different bulletin parts.

5. An unusual passage within its section

A is the least similar to its section centroid among 35 retained Public Policy passages, with cosine similarity 0.23. The model groups it with quantitative and technical study. This is a useful lead for reading across disciplinary boundaries.

6. “Credit” occupies several semantic regions

A text search for “credit” and its word endings finds 140 passages across seven modeled topics. Most fall in Credits and placement (67), Degrees, transfer and services (41), or Academic status and procedures (22). The query therefore spans degree rules, credit transfer, and academic standing contexts rather than one semantic neighborhood.

Design description · 200–300 words

Methods and interpretation

The 2021–22 DKU Undergraduate Bulletin was segmented into paragraphs and short policy blocks. PDF heading typography supplied chapter, section, and subsection labels, while each passage retained its source page. Of 3,716 candidate text blocks, repeated passages, brief table cells, and malformed fragments were removed, leaving 1,109 passages. The mean length is 79 words. Section counts and mean TF-IDF terms provide the two corpus overviews.

Each passage was encoded with the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence model and its vector was normalized. UMAP projected these vectors to two dimensions using cosine distance, 15 neighbors, a minimum distance of 0.15, and random seed 401. K-means grouped the original 384-dimensional vectors into ten clusters with 20 initializations. Topic names were assigned after comparing representative passages with characteristic TF-IDF terms. The names are descriptive summaries of the fitted groups.

In Figure 1, position represents the UMAP projection, color marks topic, and point area reflects passage length. Clicking a point opens the original text, hierarchy, page link, and five nearest passages ranked by cosine similarity in the original vector space. Search highlights text matches, while topic and section controls restrict the map. Figure 2 places formal sections in rows and semantic topics in columns. Cell color encodes the within-section topic proportion, and its tooltip reports both count and proportion. Selecting a cell highlights the corresponding map points, while selecting a point marks its matrix cell. This coordination exposes cross-section themes, including the distribution of credit rules, and supports inspection of specific source passages.
