Design record · 170 words

Design Justification

Population is mapped to horizontal bar length because it is a ratio variable with a meaningful zero, making proportional differences easy to compare. Average temperature is placed on a separate aligned horizontal axis: position supports precise comparison while avoiding the misleading implication that zero degrees means an absence of temperature. Region, a nominal variable, is represented with four distinct colors that do not imply rank. Development level is ordinal, so Low, Medium, and High are encoded with progressively larger circles and reinforced by grouped row labels.

The two aligned views share city rows, allowing the reader to compare population and climate without forcing measurements with different units onto one scale. Cities are grouped by development level and then ordered by population to reveal patterns within and across levels. Region color is repeated in both the population bars and temperature markers, while tooltips provide exact values and city identity. I hope viewers can quickly notice geographic clusters, population contrasts, and whether warmer or cooler cities appear concentrated within particular development levels.

Data & method

Four measurements, one coordinated reading.

The supplied dataset contains twelve observations. Numeric fields are parsed during CSV loading; ordinal and nominal fields are validated against the documented categories before rendering.
